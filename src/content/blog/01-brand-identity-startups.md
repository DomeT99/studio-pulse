Your brand is not your logo. It's the gut feeling someone gets when they hear your name. For startups racing toward an MVP, branding often sits in the "we'll figure it out later" bucket. That delay costs more than you think.

## First impressions compound

Investors see hundreds of pitch decks. The ones that stick have a coherent visual world — not just a product screenshot on a white slide. A deliberate identity signals that you understand your audience and take your own company seriously. It doesn't need to be expensive. It needs to be intentional.

## Consistency builds trust

When your website, app, social presence, and investor materials all share the same visual language, you're sending one message: *we know who we are*. That coherence reduces cognitive load. People trust what they can understand at a glance.

## Start simple, stay flexible

We recommend three things for early-stage teams:

1. **Name and tagline** — clear, ownable, easy to spell.
2. **Color system** — 3–5 colors you commit to for at least 12 months.
3. **Type pair** — one display font, one workhorse. No more than two.

The goal isn't to look like a Fortune 500 company on day one. It's to look like the most confident version of what you already are.
