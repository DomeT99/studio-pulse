Every scroll on Instagram, every refresh of a website, every app opening — your users are surrounded by motion. If your brand stands still, it feels outdated. Not because motion is trendy, but because static has become the anomaly.

## Motion is not decoration

A logo that animates on hover. A button that responds to press. A page transition that feels intentional. These aren't flourishes — they're functional. Motion communicates state, hierarchy, and affordance more efficiently than any static element can.

## The psychology of movement

Our peripheral vision is attuned to change. A subtle animation in the corner of the screen — a notification badge, a progress indicator — captures attention without demanding it. Used well, motion guides focus. Used poorly, it distracts. The difference is intent.

## Where to start

You don't need a full animation team. Start small:

- **Logo animation** — a 2-second intro or hover state. One and done.
- **Page transitions** — simple fades between routes (browser-native View Transitions API makes this free).
- **Scroll-triggered reveals** — content that fades in as the user scrolls. Instant premium feel.
- **Micro-interactions** — button hovers, form focus states, loading spinners. Small moments, big impact.

Start with one. Ship it. Then iterate. Motion is a muscle — the more you use it, the stronger your instincts become.
